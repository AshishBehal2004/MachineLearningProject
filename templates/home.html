A deep dive into credit card fraud detection, exploring what makes this problem hard, how machine learning approaches it, and what the numbers actually mean.

The Problem

Every day, millions of credit card transactions happen. The vast majority are completely legitimate. But buried within that volume is a tiny fraction of fraudulent activity, and catching it without flagging innocent people is genuinely difficult.

This project uses a real dataset of 284,807 transactions from European cardholders. Only 492 of them are fraud. That's not a typo, the dataset is heavily imbalanced by design, because that's what real-world fraud data looks like.

The Approach

Rather than optimizing for accuracy, this model is evaluated on ROC-AUC score which is a metric that measures how well the model separates fraud from legitimate transactions across all possible thresholds.

A Random Forest classifier was trained with a custom detection threshold of 0.2, meaning the model flags a transaction as suspicious if it assigns even a 20% probability of fraud. This reduces missed fraud at the cost of some false alarms, which is the right tradeoff in a financial context.
